## Dataset
Database: DVD Rental Database
Source: https://neon.com/postgresql/getting-started/sample-database

## JOIN Used

This assignment mainly uses **INNER JOIN** because the required data exists only when matching records are present in both tables. For some questions, multiple INNER JOINs were used to connect several related tables.

The ER Diagram of the DVD Rental database is included in the screenshots folder.

### Question 1
Displayed the customer's first name, last name, email, city, and country by joining the `customer`, `address`, `city`, and `country` tables.

### Question 2
Displayed every payment with the customer name, film title, and amount paid by joining the `payment`, `customer`, `rental`, `inventory`, and `film` tables.

### Question 3
Same solution as Question 2 because the question was repeated.

### Question 4
Calculated the total amount spent by each customer using `SUM()`. The results were grouped by customer, sorted in descending order, and limited to the top 10 customers.

### Question 5
Displayed each film with its category and rental rate by joining the `film`, `film_category`, and `category` tables.

### Question 6
Displayed all actors who appeared in each film by joining the `actor`, `film_actor`, and `film` tables.

### Question 7
Counted the number of films in each category using `COUNT()` and `GROUP BY`.

### Question 8
Calculated the total revenue generated by each category by joining the `category`, `film_category`, `film`, `inventory`, `rental`, and `payment` tables and using `SUM(payment.amount)`.

### Question 9
Found customers who rented more than 20 films using `COUNT()`, `GROUP BY`, and `HAVING`.

### Question 10
Calculated the rental revenue generated by each city by joining the `city`, `address`, `customer`, and `payment` tables and using `SUM(payment.amount)`.

### Bonus Challenge
Actor → Film_Actor → Film → Inventory → Rental → Payment
This path connects actors with the payments made for rentals of the films in which they appeared.

## Business Insights

1. A small number of customers contribute a large portion of the total rental revenue.
2. Some film categories generate significantly more revenue than others, showing higher customer demand.
3. Revenue differs from city to city, indicating that customer activity is not the same across all locations.
